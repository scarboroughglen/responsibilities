## Background

At the request of Scarborough Glen Condominium IV owners, the following **unaudited Statement of Income and Liabilities** has been prepared. This schedule incorporates the Condominium's general-ledger activity for the period **January 1, 2026 through June 30, 2026**, together with projected future repair and replacement obligations identified in the **Becht Engineering Capital Reserve Study (2026)** and the **Falcon Engineering Report (2022)**.

The Becht study evaluates the common property of Scarborough Glen as a whole; the Falcon report separates obligations by entity. The figures below reflect the portion allocated to Condominium IV (98 units) using the per-entity quantity ratios derived from the Falcon report.

This document is intended to help owners understand this Condominium's financial position, current reserve funding, and long-term repair and replacement obligations, and to support the Board and management in reserve planning. It has been prepared by the Condominium's Treasurer and is unaudited; it is not intended to represent audited financial statements in accordance with U.S. GAAP, and no assurance is expressed on the projections.

This statement is issued twice a year: a mid-year edition (January–June), issued in the fall with the proposed budget, and a full-year edition (January–December), issued in the spring. This is the mid-year 2026 edition; the next edition will cover all of 2026.

## Funding Gap Analysis

There are two ways to read the gap. **(A)** is what the reserve plan actually requires the Association to do; **(B)** is the industry health metric showing how far behind the community fell before the plan was adopted.

### A. What the plan requires (cash-flow basis)

| Measure | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Reserve cash on hand at 6/30/26 (Webster Reserve + PCSB CD + Roof Fund) | $132,147.88 |
| Annual contribution required (all three funds, 2027) | **~$171,500/yr (~$145.83/unit/mo)** |
| — Long-Term Maintenance | $135,100/yr ($114.88/unit/mo) |

| Emergency Reserve — target | $125,000 (uninsured exposure; see note below) |
| Operating Reserve — floor (3 months operating expense @ $39,287/mo, from the 2027 budget) | $117,860 |
| Long-Term Maintenance | Kept solvent every year (peak need ~$3.3M for the 2049 siding) |
| Total replacement obligation (reserve plan, 2027–2050 horizon) | $3,594,809 |

> **How the Emergency Reserve target was set.** Under the First Amendment to the HOA Declaration, the HOA insures Condo IV's buildings (§22.1) and carries out rebuilding after a fire or other casualty (§22.2). This target assumes the HOA also bears the policy deductible. A building loss is the insurer's job, not this reserve's, so the target covers only what insurance won't pay in a bad year:
>
> | Uninsured event | Allowance |
> |---|---:|
> | One sudden structural failure at a building (foundation settlement, rot behind the siding, water intrusion, deck structure) | $75,000 |
> | Clustered urgent safety repairs (window wells, stoop steps, railings). One window-well rebuild cost $12,428 in H1 2026 | $25,000 |
> | Rebuild cost above insurance proceeds, e.g. code upgrades (NY RPL §339-cc makes this a Condo common expense) | $25,000 |
> | **Target** | **$125,000** |
>
> The fund is built from contributions. With $23,100 set aside from 2026, the plan's minimum schedule reaches about $125,075 at the end of 2030, after about $99,570 of further contributions, and then stops collecting. The proposed 2027 budget funds it fully in 2027. This replaces the earlier $300,000 placeholder, which was framed as "worst-case building replacement", a risk the HOA's master policy covers. Three things should be confirmed: the HOA master policy (Community Associations Underwriters, CAU512582-8) covers all Condo IV buildings at replacement cost; the deductible falls on the HOA; and the policy includes ordinance-and-law (code upgrade) coverage. Without that coverage, raise the shortfall allowance to about $50,000, for a target of about $150,000. If the deductible instead falls on Condo IV, add it for each building a single storm could damage.

*The reserve plan was re-based in October 2026 to start January 1, 2027, from the projected December 31, 2026 reserve cash of $176,604 (Emergency $23,100, Long-Term Maintenance $91,605, Operating $61,899), so its 2027 figures match the 2027 budget. This plan funds each obligation as it comes due; it does **not** require a large lump sum on hand today. The reserve balance rises and falls as bills are paid — it never needs to sit at the "fully funded" figure in section B.*

> *The half-year data supports the $36,503/month operating basis used by the plan: actual all-in operating spend (the HOA pass-through plus Condo IV's own costs) was $221,926.48 over six months, or **$36,987.75/month** — within 1.3% of the planned figure. The plan, re-based to 2027, now uses $39,287/month, the 2027 budget's monthly operating spend including the HOA pass-through.*

### 2. Roof assessment installments — receivable and designation

The 2025 roof replacement was paid by the Condominium, partly through a bank loan. 45 owners prepaid their share ($5,673). The other 53 chose a 3-year payment plan of $174.52 a month for 36 months, $6,282.72 per unit in total, including about $610 of interest. The bank loan was paid off on July 17, 2026, but the payment-plan owners continue to owe their installments to the Condominium.

- **Receivable.** Assuming the plan runs March 2025 – February 2028, about **$185,000** was still owed at June 30, 2026 (20 installments × $174.52 × 53 units), falling to about **$129,500** at December 31, 2026. These are estimates; the Condominium's receivable ledger is the authoritative figure. A unit that sells or pays off early stops paying, so these are maximums.
- **Designation.** Beginning with the 2027 budget, every installment collected is transferred to the Long-Term Maintenance reserve as a separately identified transfer. The installments repay Condominium cash that paid for the roof, so they return to the fund for future building work. None of this money funds operating costs.
