## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Based on review of your governing documents (Condo IV By-Laws, HOA/Association Documents, and the **First Amendment to the HOA Declaration and By-Laws**), here is a detailed analysis of each item in your responsibility listing.

**Revised October 2026** to incorporate the First Amendment (`pdf/hoa_amendment_1.pdf`). ## KEY GOVERNING PRINCIPLES FROM BYLAWS

### First Amendment to Declaration and By-Laws of Scarborough Glen HOA (executed Jan 15, 2001; recorded Westchester County Feb 14, 2001, Control No. 410370038)

**§1(d) - "Common Areas" (maintained by the HOA under §13 and §19.3):**
> "(i) the portion of the land comprising the Condominiums, (ii) all installations outside the Units and located in or on the land of the Condominiums for common service such as heat, power, light, gas, telephone, television and water, (iii) all sewer, water and drainage pipes, appurtenance and facilities located outside of the buildings of the Condominiums or under the concrete slabs thereof and in or on the lands of the Condominiums, (iv) all parking areas located on the lands of the Condominiums, (v) all walks and driveways, (vi) all private roads on the Property and (vi) any portion of pipes, wires, conduits, cable TV and public utility lines which is located in one Unit and services another Unit or more than one Unit."

**§13 - What the Condos keep:**
> "...the Condominiums, and not the Association, shall operate, manage, repair and maintain all other areas comprising their respective Common Elements, including, without limitation, all buildings, any foundations, columns, girders, beams, supports or bearing walls of the Units, any portion of the exterior walls or walls and partitions dividing the Units from other Units, and any terraces, decks, porches and exterior stairs."

**Other provisions that bear on responsibility:**
- **§13 - HOA can compel a Condo**: The HOA Board, by majority vote, may require a Condo to perform maintenance needed for the "continued aesthetics and/or the health, safety and/or welfare of the Community". If the Condo doesn't, the HOA may do the work and the Condo must reimburse it.
- **§19.2 - Negligence**: If a Member's negligence, misuse or neglect makes repairs to the Recreational Facilities or Common Areas necessary, that Member pays the full cost (except what insurance covers).
- **§19.3 - Standard**: The Recreational Facilities and the Condos' Common Elements must be kept in "first-class condition". The HOA Board maintains the Recreational Facilities and Common Areas; each Condo board maintains "the balance of the Common Elements".
- **§19.4 - Self-help**: If a Unit Owner or Condo board fails to do work it is obligated to do, the HOA Board may do it and charge them. A charge to a Condo is split among its Units by common-element percentage and becomes a lien on each Unit.
- **§20.3 - Equal shares**: Association Expenses are "allocated and assessed to and borne by the Members, in equal shares" (136 Members).
- **§22.1 - Insurance**: The HOA Board obtains all insurance the Condominium Documents require the Condos to carry. The cost is an Association Expense "borne equally by the Members".
- **§22.2 - Casualty**: The HOA Board performs the Condo boards' obligations to repair or rebuild after fire or other casualty (unless 75%+ of Units are destroyed and 75% of Members vote not to rebuild).
- **§12 (new Declaration Article IX) - Architectural Control**: No exterior change to the Units or Condo Common Elements, including landscaping, may be made without written approval of the HOA Board or its architectural committee. If the Board doesn't decide within 60 days, the change is deemed approved.
- **§6.2 - Cross-easement**: Each Condo's Common Elements are subject to an easement in favor of the other Condos to use and maintain shared pipes, ducts, cables, wires and utility lines.

**Document hierarchy:** The HOA Declaration and its First Amendment are recorded covenants that run with all the land, Condo land included. Condo IV was formed after the amendment ("as and when formed", §1(f)). Where a Condo's own bylaws assign one of the §1(d) Common Areas to that Condo's Board of Managers, this analysis treats the HOA Declaration as controlling between the HOA and the Condo. The Condo bylaws still govern the split between a Condo board and its own Unit Owners. *This is a reading of the documents, not a legal opinion; confirm with association counsel.*

### Condo IV Bylaws - Section 6 (Repairs and Maintenance):
- **Common Elements**: Board of Managers maintains exterior walls, courtyard floors, fences, roof, and roof members. *(Scarborough Glen has no courtyard floors and no Condo privacy fences, so in practice those two items don't apply. All fencing is perimeter or common fencing; see Item 9.)*
- **Limited Common Elements**: Unit Owner maintains (decks, porches, terraces, entrances) EXCEPT structural repairs and painting
- **Within Units**: Unit Owner maintains everything inside the unit including windows, doors, skylights (except exterior re-painting)
- **Pipes/Wires**: Board maintains any pipes/wires that serve multiple units or are in common elements. **[AMENDED]** Between the HOA and the Condo, pipes and wires that sit in one Unit and serve another or several Units, and pipes outside the building or under the slab, are HOA Common Areas (Amendment §1(d)(iii) and (vi)). The Condo board keeps only common-element lines inside the building that don't fit those definitions.
- **Negligence Rule**: If damage is caused by unit owner negligence, that owner pays even if it's normally a Condo expense

### HOA/Association Documents:
- **Underground utilities**: Association responsible for underground plumbing, mechanical, utility lines outside buildings
- **Distribution lines**: Association maintains trunk systems serving multiple condos (storm drains, sanitary mains, water mains, electric under roadways)
- **Building laterals and feeders [AMENDED]**: The earlier version of this analysis said each Condo maintains its own parcel-specific laterals and feeders. No governing-document source was cited for that rule, and it conflicts with Amendment §1(d)(ii)-(iii): **all** sewer, water and drainage pipes outside the buildings or under the slabs, and all common-service installations outside the Units, are HOA Common Areas. Laterals and feeders are HOA responsibility up to the building wall or slab.
- **Grounds**: Association maintains common areas including front, side, rear yards of all units. The amendment confirms this: the land of the Condos is HOA Common Area (§1(d)(i)).
- **Watering responsibility**: Unit Owners water first 25 feet of lawn
- **Snow removal**: Association clears parking spaces and driveways

### HOA ITEMS

#### 1. Insurance - Master Policy
**Listing Says**: HOA (with note about 1st Amendment)
**Bylaws Say**: ✓ CORRECT - First Amendment §22.1 made HOA responsible for all insurance
**Comment**: Properly assigned. HOA maintains master policy covering all buildings.
- **Spreadsheet asks "Should this be separate by condo?"**: **No.** §22.1 says the cost of all such insurance "shall constitute an Association Expense and shall be borne equally by the Members", i.e. split equally across all 136 units, not by Condo.
- **Casualty rebuilding (§22.2)**: After a fire or other casualty, the HOA Board, not the Condo board, carries out the Condo Documents' repair and rebuilding obligations. This is not currently a line in the spreadsheet; it should be added.
- **Open**: The amendment does not say who pays the **deductible** on a claim for a Condo building. This needs a Board policy or a legal opinion.
- **Reserve implication**: Because the HOA insures the Condo buildings and runs rebuilding, a Condo Emergency Reserve shouldn't be sized to replace a building. It should cover only uninsured exposure: sudden structural failures, clustered safety repairs, and rebuild cost above insurance proceeds (NY RPL §339-cc). Condo IV's target was reset on that basis from a $300,000 placeholder to **$125,000, built over 5 years**, assuming the deductible falls on the HOA (see `Condo_IV_Statement_of_Income_and_Liabilities.md`). Condos I-III were re-sized the same way, also over 5 years. Each target is one structural failure at a typical building + clustered safety repairs + above-proceeds shortfall, scaled to building size per Falcon: **Condo I $147,500** (two 9-unit multi-family buildings), **Condo II $132,500** (two 5-6 unit rows), **Condo III $112,500** (three 3-unit townhouses). These land close to the old placeholders, because a single building failure costs about the same however many units share it. Small condos carry a much larger uninsured exposure per unit ($8,000-$12,500/unit, vs. about $1,275/unit for Condo IV).

**Listing Says**: Condo IV Sprinklers (Condo); Common Area Sprinklers (HOA)

**Comment**: **This allocation requires careful reconsideration based on bylaw analysis.**

**CRITICAL BYLAW PROVISIONS:**

**From Association Documents (Page 82):**
> "The Association will be responsible for grounds maintenance and landscaping of the Common Areas, **including the front, side and rear yards of all Units**."

**From HOA Budget - Line Items:**
- Item (4): "Water Sprinkler System & Pool" = $1,000 (HOA expense)
- Item (21): "Sprinkler Maintenance" = $2,500 (HOA expense)

**From Association Documents (Page 82):**
> "The Association is responsible for **watering any shrubs and ornamental trees on the Limited Common Elements**."

**ANALYSIS OF RESPONSIBILITY:**

The key question is: **What does "Condo IV Sprinklers" actually refer to?**

**Grounds vs. Building Infrastructure Distinction:**
- **Utility lines** (water, sewer, electric) = Serve the BUILDINGS → Can be Condo-specific (parcel laterals)
- **Sprinkler systems** = Water the GROUNDS/LAWNS → Part of grounds maintenance

**Since HOA is responsible for ALL grounds maintenance** (including lawns around all units), the **sprinkler system that waters those grounds should be HOA responsibility**.

**PROPER ALLOCATION SHOULD BE:**

| Sprinkler Location | Current Assignment | Correct Assignment | Reasoning |
|-------------------|-------------------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Pool area sprinklers** | HOA | **HOA** ✓ | Pool is HOA recreational facility |
| **Clubhouse area sprinklers** | HOA | **HOA** ✓ | Clubhouse is HOA property |
| **Roadway/common area sprinklers** | HOA | **HOA** ✓ | Common areas maintained by HOA |
| **Lawns around Condo IV buildings** | Condo (?) | **HOA** ⚠️ | HOA maintains "front, side and rear yards of all Units" |
| **Lawns between Condo IV buildings** | Condo (?) | **HOA** ⚠️ | HOA maintains grounds |
| **Limited Common Element yards** | Condo (?) | **HOA** ⚠️ | HOA waters shrubs/trees on Limited Common Elements |

**THE UNIT OWNER'S 25-FOOT WATERING OBLIGATION:**

**From Association Documents (Page 82):**
> "Each Unit Owner is responsible for the watering of the first 25 feet of his/her lawn, including front lawns and back/side yards."

**CRITICAL CLARIFICATION:** This obligation does NOT mean owners maintain the sprinkler system in that area.

**What the 25-foot rule means:**
- **Supplemental manual watering** (with hose/watering can) during hot summer months
- **Emergency manual watering** if sprinkler system fails or is inadequate
- **Preventing lawn death** through neglect

**What it does NOT mean:**
- Owners don't own the sprinkler infrastructure
- Owners don't repair broken sprinkler heads
- Owners don't maintain the irrigation system

**Example Scenario:**
1. **Sprinkler head breaks** in front yard (within 25 feet of unit)
2. **HOA repairs the sprinkler head** (part of grounds maintenance)
3. **While waiting for repair**, unit owner must manually water to prevent lawn death
4. **If owner fails to water** and lawn dies → Owner charged for re-seeding (even though HOA's sprinkler broke)

**POSSIBLE INTERPRETATION OF "CONDO IV SPRINKLERS":**

The only scenarios where Condo IV would be responsible for sprinklers:

**Option 1: Building-Attached Irrigation** (Unlikely)
- Roof irrigation systems
- Planters attached to building exterior
- Something that's part of the building structure itself
- These would be "building maintenance" not "grounds maintenance"

**Option 2: Separate Condo-Specific System** (Possible)
- If Condo IV installed a completely separate irrigation system beyond what HOA provides
- Similar to owner-installed landscaping (becomes owner/condo responsibility)
- Would need to be documented in Condo IV records

**Option 3: Misallocation** (Most Likely)
- The listing incorrectly assigns lawn/grounds sprinklers to Condo
- Should be HOA for all grounds irrigation
- Only "Common Area Sprinklers" (pool, clubhouse, roads) correctly identified as HOA

**BUDGET EVIDENCE STRONGLY SUPPORTS HOA RESPONSIBILITY:**

The HOA budget includes:
- **$1,000** for "Water Sprinkler System & Pool" (operating costs)
- **$2,500** for "Sprinkler Maintenance" (repairs/upkeep)

**Budget Footnote (Item 4):**
> "Usage is based on **40 sprinkler heads** producing 4 gallons per minute operating 20 minutes per day for 120 days"

This shows HOA operates and maintains a comprehensive sprinkler system throughout the development.

**INFRASTRUCTURE OWNERSHIP PRINCIPLE:**

Following the utility allocation model (Item 38):
- **Underground sprinkler mains** (trunk distribution) → HOA (serves multiple areas)
- **Sprinkler laterals/heads** serving lawns → HOA (part of grounds maintenance HOA performs)

**Unlike utility lines** (which serve buildings and can be Condo-specific), **sprinkler systems serve the grounds**, which HOA maintains.

**CONCLUSION & RECOMMENDATION:**

**Current Assignment Appears INCORRECT**

Unless "Condo IV Sprinklers" refers to a very specific building-attached or Condo-installed supplemental system (which should be documented), **ALL lawn and grounds irrigation should be HOA responsibility**.

**Recommended Corrected Allocation:**
- **Pool/Clubhouse/Roadway Sprinklers** → HOA ✓ (currently correct)
- **Lawn/Grounds Sprinklers** (including around Condo IV buildings) → **Should be HOA** (currently appears to be assigned to Condo)

**Action Items:**
1. **Review the "Sprinkler Memo"** referenced in your notes to understand what "Condo IV Sprinklers" specifically refers to
2. **Check Condo IV budgets** - Do they include sprinkler maintenance costs? (If not, it's HOA)
3. **Examine original construction documents** - Were sprinklers installed as part of building construction (unlikely) or site development (HOA)?
4. **Verify current practice** - Who actually repairs broken sprinkler heads around Condo IV buildings?

**If no evidence exists for a separate Condo-specific sprinkler system, this allocation should be corrected to HOA.**

### Bylaw-Specific Clarifications to Note:
1. **Negligence Rule**: If unit owner negligence causes damage to common elements, that owner pays for repair even though it would normally be Condo/HOA expense. Important for enforcement. First Amendment §19.2 applies the same rule to HOA Recreational Facilities and Common Areas.

2. **HOA Enforcement Powers (First Amendment §13, §19.4)**:
   - The HOA Board may require a Condo to do maintenance needed for community aesthetics, health or safety. If the Condo doesn't, the HOA may do it and bill the Condo.
   - If any Unit Owner or Condo board fails to do required work, the HOA may do it and charge them. Charges to a Condo are split among its Units by common-element percentage and become liens.

3. **Re-painting vs. Replacement**: 
   - Door/window REPLACEMENT → Unit Owner
   - Exterior surface RE-PAINTING of doors/windows → Condo (when building is repainted)
   - This distinction is captured in Item 14 but could be clarified for doors too

4. **Lawn Watering vs. Sprinkler System Maintenance - CRITICAL DISTINCTION**:
   - **Unit Owner's 25-foot watering obligation** = Supplemental MANUAL watering (hose/watering can) to prevent lawn death
   - **Sprinkler System Maintenance** = HOA responsibility (part of grounds maintenance)
   - **Key Point**: Owner must manually water if sprinkler system fails, but owner does NOT repair/maintain the sprinkler system itself
   - **If sprinkler breaks and owner fails to manually water** → Owner charged for re-seeding the dead lawn
   - **Budget Evidence**: HOA budget includes $1,000 for sprinkler water and $2,500 for sprinkler maintenance
   - **Bylaw Evidence**: "Association will be responsible for grounds maintenance and landscaping of the Common Areas, including the front, side and rear yards of all Units"; First Amendment §1(d)(i) makes the land of the Condominiums an HOA Common Area

5. **Landscaping Modifications**: Once a homeowner modifies landscaping (even with approval), ongoing maintenance is theirs, not HOA's. This is captured in Item 50. Approval itself runs through Declaration Article IX (First Amendment §12), with a 60-day deemed-approval clock.

6. **Owner-Installed Improvements vs. Original Construction**: Critical distinction for patios, decks, and landscaping:
   - **Original/unmodified** = Condo or HOA maintains (depending on location)
   - **Owner-modified** (even with ARC approval) = Owner maintains going forward
   - This applies to patios, decks, fencing, landscaping, etc.

7. **Dryer Vents - Split Responsibility**:
   - Interior vent cleaning = Owner
   - Exterior cap (part of building envelope) = Condo
   - This correctly reflects that the vent system penetrates through a Common Element (the exterior wall)

8. **Sprinkler System Infrastructure vs. Watering Obligation**:
   - **Sprinkler pipes, heads, valves, distribution** = HOA (grounds maintenance infrastructure)
   - **Water costs and operation** = HOA (per budget)
   - **Supplemental manual watering** = Unit Owner obligation (first 25 feet)
   - **Grounds vs. Building principle**: Sprinklers serve the grounds, which the HOA maintains. Under the First Amendment, building utility lines outside the building are HOA as well.

9. **"Outside the footprint = HOA" and its exceptions [AMENDED]**: The First Amendment largely supports the simple framework in `simple_interpretation.md`. Land, walks, driveways, parking, and pipes outside the buildings or under the slabs are all HOA (§1(d)). The stated exception is **terraces, decks, porches and exterior stairs**, which stay Condo even though they are outside the footprint (§13).
