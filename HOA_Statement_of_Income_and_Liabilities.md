## Background

At the request of Scarborough Glen owners, the following **unaudited Statement of Income and Liabilities** has been prepared for the **Master Homeowners Association (HOA)**. This schedule incorporates the Association's general-ledger activity for the period **January 1, 2026 through August 3, 2026**, together with the projected future repair and replacement obligations identified in the **Becht Engineering Capital Reserve Study (2026)** and the **Falcon Engineering Report (2022)** as allocated to the HOA.

Unlike the four condominium associations, the HOA owns **no residential buildings**. It maintains the common property serving all **136 units** — grounds and landscaping, roadways and site paving, the clubhouse and pool complex, fencing, site lighting, signage, and shared utilities — and it collects the HOA dues from the condominium associations that fund those services. There is therefore **no master-association pass-through** on the HOA's books; the dues it receives are core operating income.

This document is intended to help Association owners understand the HOA's financial position, current reserve funding, and long-term repair and replacement obligations, and to support the Board and management in reserve planning. It has been prepared by management and is **unaudited**; it is not intended to represent audited financial statements in accordance with U.S. GAAP, and no assurance is expressed on the projections.

## Funding Gap Analysis

There are two ways to read the gap. **(A)** is what the reserve plan actually requires the Association to do; **(B)** is the industry health metric showing how well-funded the community is relative to the accrual benchmark.

### A. What the plan requires (cash-flow basis)

| Measure | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Reserve cash on hand (Webster Money Market) | $366,645.56 |
| Annual contribution required (all funds, Year 1) | **~$112,100/yr (~$69/unit/mo)** |
| Emergency Reserve — target (largest common asset: clubhouse / pool) | $200,000 |
| Operating Reserve — floor (3 months operating expense) | ~$128,000 |
| Long-Term Maintenance | Kept solvent every year (peak need ~$330K for the 2036 asphalt paving) |
| Total 25-year replacement obligation | $1,041,818 |

*This plan funds each obligation as it comes due; it does **not** require a large lump sum on hand today. The reserve balance rises and falls as bills are paid — it never needs to sit at the "fully funded" figure in section B.*

### B. Industry health check (accrual basis — diagnostic only)

| Measure | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Fully Funded Balance benchmark (straight-line accrual to date) | $557,180 |
| Reserve on hand | $366,645.56 |
| **Percent funded** | **~65.8%** *(CAI "fair" band is 30–70%; the HOA sits just below the "strong" 70%+ threshold)* |

*Percent funded measures how the community stands against the accrual benchmark. It is a diagnostic — **not** an amount that must be held in cash. Unlike the condominium associations, the HOA is comparatively well-funded.*

### Priorities

The nearest-term items — **pool shell resurfacing and the split-rail fence repair fund (2027)**, then **pool mechanical, cover, and clubhouse gutters (2028)** — should be funded first. The largest single obligation, **asphalt road paving (~$330,000 in 2036)**, together with the 2033 cluster (sidewalks, street lighting, clubhouse refurbishments), should drive the Long-Term Maintenance contribution schedule. Signage and other placeholder costs may shift as field verification is completed.
